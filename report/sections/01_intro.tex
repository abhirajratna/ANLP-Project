\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A masked diffusion model writes by unmasking: at each forward pass the decoder
commits tokens at the positions it is most confident about.
LLaDA commits $k$ of them, which is also an option in Dream,
and decoding rules such as Fast-dLLM commit every position above a confidence threshold \citep{nie2025llada,ye2025dream,chang2022maskgit,wu2025fastdllm}.
Confidence is taken as how sure the model is about a particular option,
so it is read as the chance the committed tokens are right.
However, this confidence is also subject to noise, and the largest of many such estimates
tends to be one whose error pushed it up.
This is the winner's curse \citep{andrews2024winners}, and a commit out of many masked positions is such a maximum.

Calibration of diffusion LMs, where measured, is over whole answers
\citep{yadav2026bidirectional,jiang2026bayeser} or per committed token without conditioning on
the selection \citep{asaria2026diffusiongemma}. Nor is the bias automatic: selection does not bias a correct posterior
\citep{dawid1994selection,efron2011tweedie}. We show that the gap between confidence and accuracy on committed
tokens splits exactly, when positions' errors are independent given the state, into ordinary
miscalibration and a \emph{field term}, nonzero only if
accuracy at fixed confidence depends on context (Proposition~\ref{prop:split},
\S\ref{sec:setup}). Only the field term is a winner's curse, and only an audit at the
decoder's selection can see it.

Three contributions follow.
\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{C1, a selection-conditional calibration audit} (\S\ref{sec:c1}).
  On teacher-forced traces from LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B and a 110M-parameter model that we trained,
  we measure the committed-token gap and its winner's-curse field term against how many candidates a token beat,
  with Holm-corrected tests on held-out documents.
  In the states a confidence decoder visits, the gap grows with field size for every model, and the field term grows for both instruct models on both tasks.
  For the base and 110M models the field term is near the bias of the estimator. At
  equal confidence, tokens are right less often in those states than in random masks of the same size, in every model (exploratory).
  
\item \textbf{C2, an instrument for the two errors of parallel commitment}
  (\S\ref{sec:c2}). Committing several tokens in one step incurs a dependency error (A) and an
  estimation error (B) (Eq.~\ref{eq:decomp}), computed exactly on a JSON probe with known joint
  distribution. (A) overtakes (B) only for strongly coupled slots
  ($\rho\ge\CTwoConfCrossRhoMin$) and several tokens per step, and confidence ranking raises (A),
  not (B); the model's partial reading of $\rho$ also moves (B). On real text a weak top-token proxy for (A) is near zero
  below the all-mask state.
\item \textbf{C3, a calibrated commitment policy} (\S\ref{sec:c3}). C3a commits as many
  positions as have recalibrated confidence at least $1-\alpha$, in
  confidence order, with no extra forward pass. At matched speed
  (LLaDA-8B-Instruct, GSM8K) it beats top-$k$, margin and PC-Sampler but not entropy-bounded
  decoding or the Fast-dLLM threshold it recalibrates, which it trails at 8 tokens per step
  (exploratory).

\end{itemize}
