\paragraph{The rule.}
A top-$k$ decoder fixes the number of tokens per step in advance; C3a lets calibrated
confidence set it. At each step it computes $\hat c_i=\hat\pi(\ell_i,s)$ for every masked
position, with the context-aware calibrator of \S\ref{sec:estimands} on three field
features (median $\ell$, mask rate, $\log|M|$), and commits the $n$ positions with the highest $\ell$, $n$ being the number with
$\hat c_i\ge 1-\alpha$ (at least one). Where $\hat c$ rises with $\ell$ these are exactly the
positions with $\hat c_i\ge 1-\alpha$, so $\alpha$ bounds each committed token's calibrated error;
with $\ell\times$feature terms that can fail (it holds in a fraction
\Cres{CThreeTElevenGsmMonotoneShare} of GSM8K and \Cres{CThreeTElevenMbppMonotoneShare} of MBPP
fit states). $\alpha$ is the one speed knob. Positions are taken in the model's own order of $\ell$, so
C3a changes \emph{how many} tokens are committed, not \emph{which}. It needs no extra
forward pass.

\paragraph{T11: the calibrator.}
One calibrator per task is fitted on teacher-forced decoder states ($k\in\{2,4,8\}$) of the
fit-half LLaDA-8B-Instruct traces (\Cres{CThreeTElevenGsmFitDocs} GSM8K and
\Cres{CThreeTElevenMbppFitDocs} MBPP documents) and frozen. A check inside the fit half (not
a result) puts its ECE against the raw $\sigma(\ell)$ at \Cres{CThreeTElevenGsmDevECECal} from
\Cres{CThreeTElevenGsmDevECERaw} on GSM8K and \Cres{CThreeTElevenMbppDevECECal} from
\Cres{CThreeTElevenMbppDevECERaw} on MBPP. It is then applied to free generation, which starts
from 256 masks; its fit states lie inside a reference answer. A fraction
\Cres{CThreeTTwelveGsmOutsideShare} of C3a's GSM8K steps and
\Cres{CThreeTTwelveMbppOutsideShare} of its MBPP steps have features outside the fitted range.

\paragraph{T12: matched speed.}
LLaDA-8B-Instruct generates freely on the report halves of GSM8K
(\Cres{CThreeTTwelveGsmN} problems) and MBPP (\Cres{CThreeTTwelveMbppN}): zero-shot chat prompt,
256 tokens in blocks of 32, greedy, one seed, fp16, on two RTX PRO 6000 GPUs.
C3a is compared with confidence top-$k$, Fast-dLLM's threshold \citep{wu2025fastdllm},
margin \citep{kim2025worst}, entropy-bounded \citep{benhamu2025entropy},
KLASS \citep{kim2025klass} and PC-Sampler \citep{huang2025pcsampler} (full variant, with
background token frequencies from held-out FineWeb-Edu text rather than C4).
Each decoder has one speed knob ($k$, $\tau$, $\gamma$, $\alpha$, \dots), calibrated per task on
fit-half problems only to 2, 4 and 8 tokens per step (speeds 1 to 3). Top-$k$, margin and
PC-Sampler are exact at $k=2,4,8$; the threshold, entropy-bounded, KLASS and C3a settings are
interpolated in $\log$ speed. Every knob is chosen by speed alone, never by accuracy. Speed is
aggregate tokens per step, $256N/\sum\mathrm{NFE}$ over a configuration's $N$ problems. If
KLASS cannot reach 8 tokens per step within $\pm10\%$, its fastest setting fills the speed-3
slot and that comparison reads ---. Table~\ref{tab:c3-speed} shows speed 2 (about 4 tokens
per step); speeds 1 and 3 and the cost per decoder are in the code archive, and
Appendix~\ref{sec:c3-app} has the sanity check and the speed curves. Each decoder is taken at
its setting nearest to C3a's tokens per step, within $\pm10\%$ (else ---); the accuracy
difference is paired on the shared problems, with a 95\% percentile bootstrap interval from
2,000 resamples of problems. T12 is exploratory and outside the C1 analysis of
\S\ref{sec:inference}: no multiple-testing correction is applied, and differences are reported
as estimates with intervals.
