\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Decoders.}
LLaDA and Dream \citep{nie2025llada,ye2025dream} scale masked diffusion \citep{sahoo2024mdlm}
to 7--8B parameters and commit the most confident positions, as MaskGIT did
\citep{chang2022maskgit}; we audit that rule and propose no model. Other decoders replace top-1
probability \citep{kim2025worst} with entropy, margin or stability
\citep{benhamu2025entropy,kim2025klass}, fix the cutoff \citep{wu2025fastdllm}, set it per block and step from a profiled run's confidences \citep{shen2025osdt} or
learn the policy \citep{jazbec2026learning}; they evaluate speed and accuracy, not the
calibration of the tokens they commit. C3a thresholds a probability of being right, fitted on labelled states.

\paragraph{Calibration and the winner's curse.}
Calibration is usually measured over all predictions \citep{guo2017calibration}. PC-Sampler
\citep{huang2025pcsampler} recalibrates the commit statistic for position and token frequency; we add its positional
weight as a covariate. Keeping the largest of noisy estimates biases it upward
\citep{andrews2024winners}, more so as the pool grows \citep{dalal2026beam}, but leaves a correct
posterior unbiased \citep{dawid1994selection,efron2011tweedie}. Diffusion LMs are overconfident
against autoregressive models on most tasks \citep{yadav2026bidirectional}, an entropy-based
commit order lowers their sequence-level calibration error \citep{jiang2026bayeser}, and commit
confidence tracks correctness on math but not on factual recall
\citep{asaria2026diffusiongemma}; none conditions on how many candidates a committed token beat.

\paragraph{Dependency between tokens.}
Dependency-aware decoders \citep{kim2026dapd,ringel2026demask,azangulov2026punt} and
ParallelBench \citep{kang2026parallelbench} target term (A) of Eq.~\ref{eq:decomp} and take
the marginals as accurate. The split is not ours: EB-Sampler uses the same two terms
\citep{benhamu2025entropy}, and \citet{tang2026sampler} note that sampler and model error stay
entangled; C2 computes both in nats where $p^*$ is known.

\section{Conclusion}

Confidence-based decoders use one number both to choose which tokens to commit and as the chance that they are right; checked at the decoder's own selection, the first use biases the second. Dependency error dominates parallel commits only for strongly coupled tokens, and recalibrating the commit threshold gives the cutoff an error rate, not extra accuracy.
