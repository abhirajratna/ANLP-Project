\section{Two sources of error (C2)}
\label{sec:c2}

Committing a set $S$ of masked tokens in one step samples them from the product of their
marginals. Given the context $x_{\mathrm{obs}}$, the KL from the true joint to that product
splits exactly (Eq.~\ref{eq:decomp}, \S\ref{sec:setup}) into a dependency term
(A)~$=\mathrm{TC}(x_S \mid x_{\mathrm{obs}}) = \sum_{i\in S} H(p^*_i) - H(p^*_S)$, which no
product of marginals can represent, and an estimation term
(B)~$=\sum_{i\in S}\mathrm{KL}(p^*_i \,\|\, p_{\theta,i})$, the error of the marginals
themselves \citep[cf.][]{benhamu2025entropy}. Work on parallel decoding targets (A) and takes
the marginals as accurate \citep{kim2026dapd,ringel2026demask,azangulov2026punt,kang2026parallelbench}.
We compute both exactly on a probe with known $p^*$, then test real-text proxies against it.

\subsection{A JSON probe with known $p^*$}
\label{sec:c2-probe}

% 200 schemas, 6--16 fields, 200k instances: artifacts/probe/manifest.json (n_schemas,
% fields_per_schema.min/max, train.n).  rho values: artifacts/probe/spec.json (rhos).
The probe fills the simple fields of 200 SchemaStore schemas \citep{schemastore}, 6--16 per
schema, with single-token values. Each field has a seeded Dirichlet(1) marginal, and fields
are paired: with probability $\rho$, stated in a marker at the start of the instance, the
follower is a fixed function of its leader's value, otherwise an independent draw, so $p^*$
is known exactly. We fine-tune our 110M MDLM (\S\ref{sec:instrument}) on 200k instances and
evaluate \CTwoProbeNInstances{} (\CTwoProbeNInstancesPerRho{} per
$\rho\in\{0,.25,.5,.75,1\}$); the main model is the fine-tune that never saw the marker masked
(Appendix~\ref{sec:c2-app}). Decoding starts with every value masked. At each step the rule
picks $k\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ fields, the most confident (top-1 probability) or at random; we
compute (A) and (B) for that set, with $p_{\theta,i}$ the model's probability of each allowed
value (mass off the list counts against (B)), and fill it with the true values (teacher
forcing). Terms are summed over steps, divided by the number of fields and bootstrapped over
instances (\CTwoProbeNBoot{} resamples).

\subsection{(A) overtakes (B) at high $\rho$ and $k$}
\label{sec:c2-results}

(A) is zero at $k=1$ and at $\rho=0$ and grows with both (Figure~\ref{fig:c2-crossover}).
% Crossover = the first k at which mean (A) > mean (B); none up to k = 8 at rho = 0, 0.25.
% ConfCrossRhoMin = RandCrossRhoMin = 0.5; RandCrossKat{RhoHalf,RhoThreeQuarters,RhoOne} = 8 =
% ConfCrossKat{RhoHalf,RhoThreeQuarters}; CrossKCI* = CrossK* in every main-model cell.
For $\rho\ge\CTwoConfCrossRhoMin$ it overtakes (B) at $k=\CTwoRandCrossKatRhoOne$, or at
$\rho=1$ under confidence top-$k$ already at $k=\CTwoConfCrossKatRhoOne$; each crossover is
also where the 95\% interval of (A)$-$(B) clears zero. For $\rho\le0.25$ (B) dominates up to
$k=8$. At $\rho=1$, $k=8$ (confidence), (A) is \CTwoConfAatRhoOneKEight{}
[\CTwoConfAatRhoOneKEightLo, \CTwoConfAatRhoOneKEightHi] nats per field and (B)
\CTwoConfBatRhoOneKEight{} [\CTwoConfBatRhoOneKEightLo, \CTwoConfBatRhoOneKEightHi]:
\CTwoConfShareAatRhoOneKEight\% of the total.

% Every cell where ConfB > RandB has overlapping 95% intervals (grid_ft110m-v3-visible.json,
% B_per_field); at rho = 1, k >= 2 ConfB is below RandB.
\paragraph{Confidence selection raises (A), not (B).}
The proposal expected ranking on noisy confidences to inflate (B), but where (B) is higher
than at random the intervals overlap. (A) rises instead ($\rho=1$, $k=2$:
\CTwoConfAatRhoOneKTwo{} [\CTwoConfAatRhoOneKTwoLo, \CTwoConfAatRhoOneKTwoHi] against
\CTwoRandAatRhoOneKTwo{} [\CTwoRandAatRhoOneKTwoLo, \CTwoRandAatRhoOneKTwoHi]): the ranking
commits leaders together with their followers more often than chance, which brings the
crossover forward.

\paragraph{The terms trade off.}
$\rho$ was meant to move only (A), but (B) rises too: at $k=1$, from \CTwoConfBatRhoZeroKOne{}
at $\rho=0$ to \CTwoConfBatRhoOneKOne{} at $\rho=1$. The model reads the marker only in part
(Appendix~\ref{sec:c2-app}), so a follower decoded after its leader misses the sharp marginal
the copy implies; decoded with its leader, it has a broad true marginal, which the model
matches, and the gap appears as (A) instead. At $\rho=1$, from $k=1$ to $8$, (B) falls from
\CTwoConfBatRhoOneKOne{} to \CTwoConfBatRhoOneKEight{} while (A) rises from $0$ to
\CTwoConfAatRhoOneKEight. Without fine-tuning, (B) dominates everywhere ((A) is ${\le}\,$%
\CTwoUntunedConfShareAMax\% of the total).

\subsection{Real text: proxies checked on the probe}
\label{sec:c2-text}

These exact values hold for one synthetic distribution and one 110M model, not for real text,
where $p^*$ is unknown and we use proxies. For (A), parallel--sequential disagreement: the
fraction of the $k$ most confident masked positions whose token changes if they are
committed one at a time (earlier ranks filled with the model's own tokens) instead of in one
pass. For (B), the gap $\Delta=c-a$ (positive means overconfident) and the
cross-entropy (CE) of the true token, at the same positions.
% Mask rates 0.1, 0.2, ..., 1, one state each: config.rates in both T10 JSONs.
We run the 110M model (fp32) and LLaDA-8B-Base \citep[fp16;][]{nie2025llada} on
\CTwoTTenNDocs{} fit-half FineWeb-Edu documents \citep{penedo2024fineweb}, one state at each
mask rate $0.1, 0.2, \dots, 1$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:c2-app}), and the same proxy on the probe.

\paragraph{The proxy tracks (A) weakly.}
Over the \CTwoTTenProbeNCells{} confidence-rule cells with $k\ge 2$, its Spearman
correlation with the exact (A) is \CTwoTTenProbeSpearman{} [\CTwoTTenProbeSpearmanLo,
\CTwoTTenProbeSpearmanHi] for the main model and \CTwoTTenAltProbeSpearman{}
[\CTwoTTenAltProbeSpearmanLo, \CTwoTTenAltProbeSpearmanHi] for the other. It rises with $k$,
barely with $\rho$: at $k=8$, \CTwoTTenProbeDisatRhoZeroKEight{} at $\rho=0$, where (A) is zero,
and \CTwoTTenProbeDisatRhoOneKEight{} at $\rho=1$.

\paragraph{On real text the (A) proxy is near zero.}
% "At most DisKFour at k <= 4": DisKTwo, DisKFour, LladaDisKTwo, LladaDisKFour are all <= DisKFour.
% "Delta cannot be told from zero at k = 1": [DeltaKOneLo, DeltaKOneHi] and the Llada one hold 0.
% "LLaDA does not disagree there": LladaDisKTwo/KFour/KEightRateOne are 0.
Over mask rates 0.1--0.9,\footnote{At rate 1 every input is the same all-\texttt{[MASK]}
sequence. That one state gives the 110M model nearly all of its disagreement over all ten
rates (\CTwoTTenDisAllRatesKEight{} at $k=8$); LLaDA does not disagree there.}
disagreement at $k=8$ is \CTwoTTenDisKEight{} for the 110M model and \CTwoTTenLladaDisKEight{}
for LLaDA, and at most \CTwoTTenDisKFour{} at $k\le4$. CE grows from \CTwoTTenCEKOne{} nats at
$k=1$ to \CTwoTTenCEKEight{} at $k=8$ (LLaDA: \CTwoTTenLladaCEKOne{} to
\CTwoTTenLladaCEKEight), though it includes the text's own entropy and lower ranks are harder
positions. $\Delta$ cannot be told from zero at $k=1$ and is small but positive at $k=8$:
\CTwoTTenDeltaKEight{} [\CTwoTTenDeltaKEightLo, \CTwoTTenDeltaKEightHi] and, for LLaDA,
\CTwoTTenLladaDeltaKEight{} [\CTwoTTenLladaDeltaKEightLo, \CTwoTTenLladaDeltaKEightHi].

So at 2--8 tokens per step real text looks like the probe's low-$\rho$ regime, where (B)
dominates. Two limits: the proxy sees only dependence the model has learned, so near-zero
disagreement does not show that (A) is small, and the proxies' units differ: real text gives a
direction, not a split in nats.
