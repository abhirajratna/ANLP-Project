\section{Setup and theory}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Masked diffusion decoding.}
A masked diffusion LM \citep{austin2021d3pm,sahoo2024mdlm,nie2025llada,ye2025dream} writes by
unmasking. A \emph{state} $s$ is a sequence with a set $M$ of masked positions. One forward
pass gives, at every $i\in M$, a distribution $p_{\theta,i}$ given the observed tokens
$\xobs$, with top-1 token $v_i$, \emph{confidence} $c_i=p_{\theta,i}(v_i)$ and log-odds
$\ell_i=\log\frac{c_i}{1-c_i}$, so $c_i=\sigma(\ell_i)$. The decoder \emph{commits} some
positions for good and repeats. The usual rule, from MaskGIT \citep{chang2022maskgit} and the
default of LLaDA and Dream, commits the $k$ most confident positions per step; threshold rules
such as Fast-dLLM \citep{wu2025fastdllm} commit every position above a confidence cutoff.

Committing a set $S\subseteq M$ in one step draws its tokens from the product of their
marginals. With $p^*$ the true joint of $x_S$ given $\xobs$ and $p^*_i$ its marginals,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
  \mathrm{KL}\bigl(p^* \,\|\, {\textstyle\prod_{i\in S}} p_{\theta,i}\bigr)
  = {} & \underbrace{\mathrm{TC}(x_S \mid \xobs)}_{\text{(A) dependency}} \\
  & + \underbrace{\sum_{i\in S}\mathrm{KL}(p^*_i \,\|\, p_{\theta,i})}_{\text{(B) estimation}} ,
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{TC}(x_S\mid\xobs)=\sum_{i\in S}H(p^*_i)-H(p^*_S)$ is the total correlation, zero
if and only if the tokens are conditionally independent. (A) is what no product of marginals
can represent; (B) is the error of the marginals themselves
\citep[cf.][]{benhamu2025entropy}. Section~\ref{sec:c2} estimates both. C1 asks a narrower
question about the marginals: once the decoder has chosen a token, is its confidence still an
honest probability of being right?

\paragraph{The committed-token gap.}
Let $x_0$ be the true document. Under teacher forcing (\S\ref{sec:instrument}) correctness
$a_i=\1\{v_i=x_{0,i}\}$ is observed. A commit is a selection: the committed token had the
largest $\ell$ among its candidates. We model a field of $m$ candidates as a random pool
$P\subseteq M$ of size $m$, won by $i=\arg\max_{j\in P}\ell_j$ (ties broken at random). With
$\E_m$ the average over states, pools of size $m$ and their winners, the
\emph{committed-token gap} is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta(m) = \E_m\,[\,c - a\,],
  \label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
confidence minus accuracy, so positive means overconfident.\footnote{The proposal used
accuracy minus confidence; files from that period keep the opposite sign.} $\Delta(1)$ is the
ordinary signed calibration error of a random masked position; larger $m$ stands for
committing out of a larger field, as at early steps and in long outputs.

\paragraph{The winner's curse.}
The largest of $m$ noisy estimates tends to be one whose error pushed it up: the winner's
curse \citep{efron2011tweedie,andrews2024winners}. If it acts on decoding, $\Delta(m)$ grows
with $m$, and a confidence cutoff means something different in exactly the states where it is
applied. Whether it can act depends on what calibration conditions on. Let
$\pi(\ell)=\Pr(a=1\mid\ell)$ be the ordinary calibration curve over all masked positions (the
$m=1$ population) and $\pi(\ell,s)=\Pr(a=1\mid\ell,s)$ its context-aware version.

\begin{proposition}[Winning adds nothing]
\label{prop:select}
Fix $s$. Suppose that, given $s$, each pair $(\ell_i,a_i)$ is independent of the other
positions' scores $(\ell_j)_{j\neq i}$, and that the pool and the tie-breaks are drawn
independently of all $(\ell_j,a_j)_{j\in M}$. Then the winner $i$ of $P$ satisfies
\[
  \Pr\bigl(a_i{=}1 \bigm| \ell_i,\, s,\, i=\arg\max\nolimits_{j\in P}\ell_j\bigr)=\pi(\ell_i,s).
\]
\end{proposition}

Given $\ell_i$ and $s$, whether $i$ wins depends only on the other scores, which say nothing
about $a_i$: selection does not bias a correct posterior \citep{efron2011tweedie}. This holds
however unequal the noise scales are, so heteroskedastic noise alone, the mechanism our
proposal relied on, cannot make field size informative. Selection acts only through context:

\begin{proposition}[Exact split of the gap]
\label{prop:split}
Under the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:select},
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
  \Delta(m) = {} & \underbrace{\E_m[\sigma(\ell)-\pi(\ell)]}_{\text{own miscalibration}} \\
            & + \underbrace{\E_m[\pi(\ell)-\pi(\ell,s)]}_{\text{field term}} ,
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:gapsplit}
\end{equation}
and the field term is exactly zero at $m=1$.
\end{proposition}

Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-proofs}. The first term, ordinary miscalibration reweighted
towards where winners sit, can make $\Delta(m)$ grow with $m$ with no winner's curse at all.
The \emph{field term} is nonzero only if accuracy at fixed confidence depends on context, and
winning a large pool shifts which contexts winners come from (a lone high score in a weak
field is more likely to be noise). It is the winner's curse in a corrected, testable form. If
positions of a state share errors, Proposition~\ref{prop:select} fails and
\eqref{eq:gapsplit} gains a third term, $\E_m[\pi(\ell,s)-a]$, which \S\ref{sec:instrument}
tracks.
