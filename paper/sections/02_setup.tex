\section{Setup and theory}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Masked diffusion decoding.}
A masked diffusion LM \citep{austin2021d3pm,sahoo2024mdlm,nie2025llada,ye2025dream} writes by
unmasking: in a \emph{state} $s$ with masked set $M$, one forward pass gives at each $i\in M$ a
distribution $p_{\theta,i}$ given the observed tokens $\xobs$, with top-1 token $v_i$,
\emph{confidence} $c_i=p_{\theta,i}(v_i)$ and log-odds $\ell_i=\log\frac{c_i}{1-c_i}$. The
decoder \emph{commits} positions for good: the $k$ most confident per step
\citep{chang2022maskgit}, the default of LLaDA and Dream, or all above a cutoff
\citep{wu2025fastdllm}. Committing $S\subseteq M$ in one step draws its tokens from the product
of their marginals. With $p^*$ the true joint of $x_S$ given $\xobs$ and $p^*_i$ its
marginals,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
  \mathrm{KL}\bigl(p^* \,\|\, {\textstyle\prod_{i\in S}} p_{\theta,i}\bigr)
  = {} & \underbrace{\mathrm{TC}(x_S \mid \xobs)}_{\text{(A) dependency}} \\
  & + \underbrace{\sum_{i\in S}\mathrm{KL}(p^*_i \,\|\, p_{\theta,i})}_{\text{(B) estimation}} ,
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{TC}(x_S\mid\xobs)=\sum_{i\in S}H(p^*_i)-H(p^*_S)$ is the total correlation, zero
iff the tokens are conditionally independent. (A) is what no product of marginals can
represent, (B) the error of the marginals \citep[cf.][]{benhamu2025entropy}; \S\ref{sec:c2}
estimates both, C1 only the marginals.

\paragraph{The committed-token gap.}
Under teacher forcing (\S\ref{sec:instrument}) correctness $a_i=\1\{v_i=x_{0,i}\}$ against the
true document $x_0$ is observed. A commit is a selection, so we model a field of $m$ candidates
as a random pool $P\subseteq M$, won by $i=\arg\max_{j\in P}\ell_j$ (ties broken at random).
With $\E_m$ the average over states, pools of size $m$ and their winners, the
\emph{committed-token gap} is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta(m) = \E_m\,[\,c - a\,],
  \label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
confidence minus accuracy, so positive means overconfident.\footnote{Proposal-era files use
accuracy minus confidence.} $\Delta(1)$ is the
ordinary signed calibration error of a masked position; larger $m$ stands for a larger
field, as at early steps and in long outputs.

\paragraph{The winner's curse.}
The largest of $m$ noisy estimates tends to be one whose error pushed it up
\citep{efron2011tweedie,andrews2024winners}; if that acts on decoding, $\Delta(m)$ grows with
$m$. Let $\pi(\ell)=\Pr(a=1\mid\ell)$ be the calibration curve over all masked positions (the
$m=1$ population) and $\pi(\ell,s)=\Pr(a=1\mid\ell,s)$ its context-aware version.

\begin{proposition}[Winning adds nothing]
\label{prop:select}
Fix $s$. Suppose that, given $s$, each pair $(\ell_i,a_i)$ is independent of the other
positions' scores $(\ell_j)_{j\neq i}$, and that the pool and the tie-breaks are drawn
independently of all $(\ell_j,a_j)_{j\in M}$. Then the winner $i$ of $P$ satisfies
\[
  \Pr\bigl(a_i{=}1 \bigm| \ell_i,\, s,\, i=\arg\max\nolimits_{j\in P}\ell_j\bigr)=\pi(\ell_i,s).
\]
\end{proposition}

Winning depends only on the other scores, which say nothing about $a_i$: selection does not
bias a correct posterior \citep{efron2011tweedie}, however unequal the noise scales. It acts
only through context:

\begin{proposition}[Exact split of the gap]
\label{prop:split}
Under the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:select},
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
  \Delta(m) = {} & \underbrace{\E_m[\sigma(\ell)-\pi(\ell)]}_{\text{own miscalibration}} \\
            & + \underbrace{\E_m[\pi(\ell)-\pi(\ell,s)]}_{\text{field term}} ,
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:gapsplit}
\end{equation}
and the field term is exactly zero at $m=1$.
\end{proposition}

Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-proofs}. Own miscalibration, reweighted towards where
winners sit, can make $\Delta(m)$ grow with $m$ with no winner's curse; the \emph{field term},
nonzero only if accuracy at fixed confidence depends on context, is the winner's curse in a
testable form. If positions of a state
share errors, Proposition~\ref{prop:select} fails and \eqref{eq:gapsplit} gains a third term,
$\E_m[\pi(\ell,s)-a]$ (\S\ref{sec:instrument}).
