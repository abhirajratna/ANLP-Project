\begin{abstract}
% owner: ayush.  ojas, 28 Oct: rewritten per ayush (20261028T141631Z-ayush-0ba0: lead with the
% decoder-state findings, numbers in the abstract) and cut (i).  Numbers hand-typed from the
% v1.1 regen: E1 claimed in all 7 k=4 decoder-state cells (COneConfirmedEOne counts both kinds of
% state); native/ECE 1.85..4.35 (appendix_c1, sec:c1-app-native).  Re-check at the final regen.
Masked diffusion language models commit, at each step, the positions whose top token is most
probable, so confidence picks the tokens it then describes; yet calibration is audited over
random masks. On teacher-forced traces from LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B and a 110M model of our own, we
audit calibration conditional on this selection (C1). In the states a confidence decoder
visits, the gap between a committed token's confidence and its accuracy grows with the number
of candidates it beat in all seven model and task arms (Holm-corrected, held-out documents),
and on the tokens it commits while 32--63 positions are still masked, confidence exceeds
accuracy by 1.8 to 4.4 times the random-mask calibration error (descriptive). An
exact split of the gap isolates a field term, the part that is a winner's curse: in those
states it grows for both instruct models on both tasks, and for the base models it is near the
estimator's bias. C2 computes the dependency and estimation errors of committing several
tokens at once exactly on a JSON probe with known joint distribution: dependency dominates
only for strongly coupled tokens committed several at a time, and on real text a proxy for it
is near zero. C3 commits every position whose recalibrated confidence is at least $1-\alpha$,
with no extra forward pass.
