% owner: ayush.  ojas, 28 Oct: rewritten per ayush (20261028T141631Z-ayush-0ba0: lead with the
% decoder-state findings, numbers in the abstract) and cut (i).  Numbers hand-typed from the
% v1.1 regen: E1 claimed in all 7 k=4 decoder-state cells (COneConfirmedEOne counts both kinds of
% state); native/ECE 1.85..4.35 (appendix_c1, sec:c1-app-native).  Re-check at the final regen.
Masked diffusion language models at each and every step commit the tokens at the positions where they are most confident; however, calibration remains audited over random masks. On teacher-forced traces from LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B and a 110M model of our own, we
audit calibration conditional on this selection (C1). In the states a confidence decoder
visits, the gap between a committed token's confidence and its accuracy grows with the number
of candidates it beat in all seven model and task arms (Holm-corrected, held-out documents),
and on the tokens it commits while 32--63 positions are still masked, confidence exceeds
accuracy by 1.8 to 4.4 times the random-mask calibration error (descriptive).
On splitting this gap, we isolate a field term, the part that is a winner's curse: in those states it grows for both instruct models on both tasks, though less with an additive calibrator, and on MBPP a shared-error term grows more; for the base models it is near the estimator's bias.
C2 computes the dependency and estimation errors of committing several tokens at once
by using a JSON probe with a known joint distribution, showing that dependency dominates
only for strongly coupled tokens committed several at a time, and on real text a weak proxy for it is near zero below the all-mask state.
C3 commits every position whose confidence after recalibration is at least $1-\alpha$, with no extra forward pass.
Finally, in an exploratory matched-speed comparison on GSM8K it beats decoders that commit a fixed number of tokens per step by 5 to 8 points, but not a raw confidence threshold: recalibration gives the cutoff a meaning, not extra accuracy.
