\begin{abstract}
% DRAFT (ojas fallback, 26 Oct): ayush to review
% owner: ayush.  ~185 words.  Same sources and rules as sections/01_intro.tex; no numbers.
Masked diffusion language models decode by committing, at each step, the positions
whose top token is most probable. Confidence thus picks the tokens it then describes, so on
committed tokens it is a selected quantity, yet calibration is audited over random masks. We
study it in three parts. C1 audits calibration conditional on selection: on teacher-forced
traces from LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B and a 110M model of our own, it measures how far committed tokens' confidence exceeds their accuracy as
a function of how many candidates they beat, and splits this gap exactly into ordinary
miscalibration and a field term, the part that is a winner's curse, on held-out documents
with Holm-corrected tests.
% TODO(results): one C1 sentence, same outcome wording as the C1 TODO in 01_intro.tex.
C2 computes the two errors of committing several tokens at once, dependency and estimation,
exactly on a synthetic JSON probe: dependency overtakes estimation only for strongly coupled
tokens committed several at a time, confidence ranking raises dependency error, not estimation
error, and for coupled tokens the two trade off as tokens per step grow. On real text a dependency proxy is near zero. C3 is a commitment policy
that commits as many positions as have recalibrated confidence at least $1-\alpha$, with no extra
forward pass.

\end{abstract}
