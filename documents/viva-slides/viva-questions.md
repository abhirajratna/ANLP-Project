# Viva question bank: C3 (Committed Confidence Calibration)

Answers use only the paper (PR #58 at d735832: v1.1 results, all seven arms, regen of 28 Oct) plus the exploratory C3 check (parth, 28 Oct). "?" marks a number not yet in the paper; do not quote it. C3 numbers: T11 from the frozen calibrators; T12 GSM8K from parth's scoring of all 659 report-half problems x 21 settings (29 Oct); MBPP ran on 121 of 250 problems before the GPU credit ran out. Section numbers: §4.1 main contrasts, §4.2 beta_z, §4.3 decoder states (E3, native gap), §4.4 controls and robustness, Appendix A.2 the C1 details and Table 4. Sign: Delta = confidence minus accuracy, positive = overconfident.

## A. Motivation and framing

**Q1. In one minute: what is the problem, and why is "selection" the issue?**
A masked diffusion LM commits the positions whose top token is most probable, so confidence chooses which tokens it then describes; on committed tokens confidence is a selected quantity, yet calibration is normally audited over random masks (Abstract, §1). The largest of many noisy estimates tends to be one whose error pushed it up (winner's curse, Andrews et al. 2024), and a commit out of many masked positions is such a maximum. We ask whether a committed token's confidence overstates accuracy more the more candidates it beat (§4).

**Q2. Calibration of diffusion LMs has been studied already. What is new?**
Prior work measures calibration over whole answers (Yadav et al.; Jiang and Park, §1, §7), or evaluates decoders on speed and accuracy, not the calibration of what they commit. None conditions on how many candidates a committed token beat (§7). We add the field-size dependence, a split of the gap that is exact when positions' errors are independent given the state, and a probe separating dependency from marginal error.

**Q3. Why three contributions (C1, C2, C3) rather than one?**
C1 asks whether the problem exists and how much is a winner's curse; C2 separates the other error of parallel commitment (dependency vs marginal, Eq. 1); C3 is a rule that uses calibrated confidence (§1). Be honest about C3 (§6, Table 3): on GSM8K at matched speed C3a is indistinguishable from a raw confidence threshold and beats the fixed-count decoders; calibration buys an interpretable alpha, not speed.

## B. Theory

**Q4. State Proposition 1 in plain words. Why does winning not bias a correct posterior?**
If, given the state, each position's (score, correctness) pair is independent of the other positions' scores, and the pool is drawn independently, then winning depends only on the other scores, which say nothing about this token's correctness. So Pr(correct | score, state, won) equals the context-aware calibration curve (§2, Appendix A.3, via weak union). Selection acts only through context. This is the known result that selection does not bias a correct posterior (Dawid 1994; Efron 2011). What we add is applying it to commits and deriving the split.

**Q5. What exactly is the "field term" and why is it the winner's curse?**
Prop. 2: Delta(m) = own miscalibration E[sigma(l) − pi(l)] + field term E[pi(l) − pi(l,s)]. The field term is nonzero only if accuracy at fixed confidence depends on context (it requires a nonzero grouping loss: necessary, not sufficient), and is exactly zero at m = 1 (§2). The estimated field(1) need not be 0, because the calibrators are fitted on all states while the averages use states with at least 32 masked positions; hence E2 is the contrast field(32) - field(1). Own miscalibration can also make Delta grow with m (winners sit at high confidence), with no winner's curse, so only the field term is the testable curse.

**Q6. What assumptions can fail, and what happens then?**
Conditional independence given the state can fail if positions share errors; then Prop. 1 fails and (3) gains a third term E[pi(l,s) − a] (§2). Also "field of m" is modelled as a random pool, not the decoder's actual candidate set. The paper checks the third term and the within-state ICC (§4.1): the ICC is nonzero in every arm (up to 0.006 base/110M, 0.036 to 0.056 instruct). Weak spot: errors are shared within states (the ICC interval excludes 0 in every arm), so Prop. 1 fails and the split has a nonzero third term E[pi(l,s) - a]; the three terms still sum to Delta exactly. At m=32 the third term is near 0 in the base and 110M cells, except LLaDA-Base decoder states (-0.008 [-0.009, -0.006], larger than that cell's E1). It is 0.055 to 0.103 on MBPP and in Dream-I GSM8K uniform states, larger than E2 in four of those five cells.

## C. C1 method and statistics

**Q7. How do the Holm families work, and what is "declared" versus "claimed"?**
E1 contrasts (Delta(32) − Delta(1)) and E2 contrasts (field(32) − field(1)) each form one Holm family over 14 cells (7 arms x uniform/decoder k=4) (§3.4, §4). Declared: 95% interval excludes 0 and the effect exceeds twice the non-determinism floor. Claimed: declared and Holm-rejected at 0.05. Result: for E1, 14 declared, 12 claimed; for E2, 14/14/14 (§4.1). Everything else is exploratory.
E2 is claimed in all 14 cells, but in the base and 110M cells |E2| < 0.005 is about what the estimator reads with no field term (0.001-0.002 at m=32 in simulation; the independent estimator reads 0.003). A claim there means "nonzero given the fitted calibrators", not a winner's curse. The two unclaimed E1 cells (LLaDA-I MBPP and 110M, uniform) are declared, with p_Holm 0.0524.
Weak spot: the floor is 0 in all three families (five repeats of 20 reserve documents were bitwise identical; instruct arms use their Base family's floor). So "declared" reduces to "interval excludes 0", and a claim reduces to the Holm rejection alone (prereg claim_rule; deviation 16c).

**Q8. Why those two calibrators, and what does it mean that the field term is "estimator-limited"?**
pi-hat(l) is isotonic in l; pi-hat(l,s) is logistic in a cubic spline of l plus state features (median l, mask rate, log|M|, step) and their products with l (§3.3). The field term lives between states, so it is only as context-aware as these features. On simulated no-field-term data the estimator read 0.001 to 0.002 at m=32 where the truth is zero (§3.5), so small base-model E2 values are not interpretable as a winner's curse.

**Q9. Bootstrap details, and what do the E2 intervals condition on?**
Document is the unit, 10,000 percentile resamples with one shared matrix per run, so contrasts use differenced draws (§3.4). Calibrators are fitted on a separate fit half and not refitted, so E2 intervals condition on the fit half and leave out the calibrators' own fitting uncertainty (§3.4, §4.1). They also do not include the estimator's null-level bias (0.001-0.002 at m=32 in simulation), which is why small base E2 values are not read as a curse. Weak spot: E2 intervals such as [0.001, 0.001] look tighter than the calibrator uncertainty justifies.

**Q10. How did you validate the pipeline, and what did simulation show about bias?**
Four simulated worlds (calibrated; uniformly miscalibrated; miscalibrated by context; shared errors) (§3.5). E1 was zero only in the first; beta_z without fixed effects was strongly negative only in the third. Findings: the field-term estimator reads high (0.001 and 0.002 at m=32 where the truth is zero); fixed effects do not fully clean beta_z in the third world; and shared errors give a negative field term (-0.007 [-0.009, -0.005]) with a positive beta_z without fixed effects, so beta_z is read only beside the sign of E2. An independent all-subsets estimator (after Dorigo; exploratory) reproduces E1 in the four base cells (LLaDA, Dream; uniform and decoder k=4). Its field terms match E2 and are no larger than its own null reading of 0.003. The exception is LLaDA decoder states (-0.0004 vs 0.004), which is not identified (§4.4).

**Q11. Why teacher forcing, and what does it cost?**
Correctness against the true document is observed only if the document is known. States are those of an always-right decoder, not those after a free decoder's mistakes, and a valid rewording counts as an error (Limitations).
Weak spot: this is where it bites hardest for instruct models on free-form answers (GSM8K, MBPP).

## D. C1 results and their reading

**Q12. Headline result? Does the gap grow with field size?**
In decoder states (k=4) E1 is positive and claimed for every arm: base 0.006 (LLaDA) and 0.015 (Dream), 110M 0.009, instruct 0.024 to 0.186 (Table 2). In random masked states the sign varies: negative and claimed for both bases and LLaDA-I GSM8K (e.g. LLaDA-Base −0.003 [−0.004, −0.002]) (§4.1). We do not pool signs. In uniform states E1 is positive and claimed for Dream-I on both tasks. LLaDA-I MBPP and 110M are declared but not claimed (p_Holm 0.0524).

**Q13. Base-model effects are tiny (0.006). Why does it matter?**
Sizes differ widely: |E1| <= 0.015 for base and 110M, up to 0.26 for instruct on MBPP (§4.1). The native gap (exploratory: c - a on tokens the teacher-forced k=4 decoder commits) at 32-63 masked positions is 0.013 to 0.018 for base/110M and 0.079 to 0.176 for instruct. That is 1.8 to 4.4 times each arm's random-mask ECE. For instruct it reaches 0.15 to 0.27 at 128-255 (§4.3). The practical size sits in instruct models.
Weak spot: the native-gap description mixes field size with lost context and tests nothing (§4.3).

**Q14. Is the growth a winner's curse?**
Only partly. The field term E2 grows in 7 of 8 instruct cells (0.013 to 0.176; Dream-I MBPP uniform 0.176 while E1 is 0.257), read as the curse: at equal confidence winners of large fields come from contexts where tokens are right less often (§4.1). The eighth instruct cell, LLaDA-I GSM8K uniform, is negative. Where the shared-error term is large (MBPP; Dream-I GSM8K uniform) the two-term reading is approximate. For base and 110M models |E2| < 0.005, comparable to estimator bias, so we do not read it as a curse; the decoder growth there is mostly own miscalibration (§4.4) (LLaDA-Base decoder states: the field term is not identified under the independent estimator).
Sensitivity (§4.4, "Calibrator form"): under the additive calibrator (no products of l with the state features) E2 keeps its sign and declared flag in 12 of 14 cells; with 5 or 13 knots, in 14 of 14. The two misses are claimed cells whose E2 the paper says it does not rely on: LLaDA-I MBPP uniform (0.013 drops to 0.000 [-0.004, 0.004]) and 110M decoder states (-0.0023 becomes +0.0013). The instruct field term is smaller under the additive form in all eight instruct cells (0 to 67% of its primary size).
Weak spot: E2's size, not only its interval, depends on the calibrator's form. The abstract scopes the mechanism correctly ("its field term does too for the instruct models"); be ready to say that in base models the growth is own miscalibration.

**Q15. Why do uniform and decoder states differ, and in what direction?**
In uniform states E1 is negative for the bases, in decoder states it flips positive (§4.1). Matched uniform states (exploratory) keep the negative sign for the bases (LLaDA −0.002), so the change comes from which positions the decoder leaves masked; for LLaDA-I GSM8K matched uniform states are already positive (0.016 [0.010, 0.022]), so partly from how many. E3 (§4.3, exploratory) compares accuracy at equal confidence and mask rate in decoder and matched uniform states. It is positive, interval excluding 0, in all 21 rows (7 arms x k = 2, 4, 8), sup-t p <= 0.002: tokens are right less often in decoder states, by 0.001 to 0.006 (base, 110M) and 0.03 to 0.06 (instruct); LLaDA-Base k=4: 0.0011 [0.0003, 0.0020]. Against the plain uniform grid the interval holds 0 for LLaDA-Base at k = 2 and 4. Teacher-forced, so this is which positions are left masked, not the decoder's own mistakes. So a calibration curve fitted on random masks of the same mask rate overstates accuracy in decoder states by about this much; C3a's calibrator is fitted on decoder states. E3 does not by itself show that this helps decoding: that is §6's question.

**Q16. Instruct versus base?**
Instruct models show much larger gaps and field terms, and a clear within-state ICC (0.036 to 0.056 vs <= 0.006) (§4.1). Weak spot: model type and text type are confounded (FineWeb-Edu for base, GSM8K/MBPP for instruct; Limitations), so "instruction tuning causes this" is not identified.

**Q17. How does the native gap relate to ECE, and which matters?**
ECE is over random masks; the native gap is c − a over tokens the decoder commits, grouped by masks at commit time. For LLaDA-I GSM8K ECE is 0.033 and the native gap at 32 to 63 masked positions 0.079 [0.071, 0.086]. Across all seven arms, at 32 to 63 masked positions, the native gap is 1.8 to 4.4x the ECE, its interval above it (§4.3, A.2; descriptive). Not at every mask count: for LLaDA-Base at 64-127 masked it is 0.007 [0.003, 0.010] against an ECE of 0.007. An ECE audit therefore under-reports commit-time overconfidence. ECE is unsigned (15-bin, equal mass, uniform states); the native gap is signed and exploratory.

**Q18. How robust is it? What about contamination and unseen text?**
On 500 time-split Wikipedia articles (post-release), the small negative uniform E1 of both bases is not reproduced (intervals hold 0), so the FineWeb result may reflect seen text, domain change or low power; the decoder-state E1 stays positive for all three models (§4.4). The 110M model, which saw neither set, shows larger E1 on the new domain (uniform states: 0.012 [0.009, 0.015] vs 0.0015 [0.00003, 0.0030]), so domain, not contamination, explains that. Contamination: without the 2 LLaDA and 1 Dream documents the screen flagged, both base models' E1 equal their main values. Knots 14 of 14, additive calibrator 12 of 14 (Q14). 4-bit against fp16 (LLaDA-Base, 198 documents): E1 changes by 0.0002 [-0.0014, 0.0018] in uniform and 0.0055 [-0.0004, 0.0115] in decoder states. Both hold 0, but the decoder-state difference is as large as that cell's E1 (0.006), and fp16 E1 on these documents is itself about 0 (0.0003), so a precision effect there is not ruled out. Decoder-state E2 changes by -0.0025 [-0.0030, -0.0021], the size of the base models' E2, which is another reason not to read base E2 as a curse.

**Q18b. Do position, token type or temperature explain the growth? (controls, Table 4)**
No, for decoder states: with each control E1 keeps its sign in every arm; the largest move is LLaDA-I MBPP under a temperature per mask-rate bin, 0.156 to 0.125 (§4.4, A.2). Most with-minus-without intervals exclude 0 because paired differences are precise, but the changes are small. In uniform states the base and 110M E1 moves by up to 0.004, the size of those effects, so there the controls matter (LLaDA-Base: token type 0.0029, temperature 0.0005, position below 0.0001).

**Q18c. What do beta_z and gamma say? (§4.2)**
beta_z without state effects excludes 0 in all 14 cells and is negative in 12; with state effects it holds 0 in 8 of those 12 (E2 positive in all 8 but LLaDA-I GSM8K uniform). That is reading (a), a field effect, small where E2 is small. The other 4 negative cells (LLaDA-Base uniform, Dream-Base decoder, LLaDA-I MBPP uniform, Dream-I GSM8K decoder) exclude 0 with state effects too, which we read as shared errors. The two positive cells (LLaDA-Base and 110M decoder states) have E2 at the estimator's bias; the 110M one has negative E2 beside positive beta_z, the pattern simulated shared errors gave. gamma (lower-ranked pool members, Eq. 3): Holm rejects 24 of 42 tests in 8 rows, all positive, all in the instruct models and Dream-Base decoder states: at equal confidence lower-ranked members are right more often than the winner, which Prop. 1 excludes when its conditions hold. Read like the ICC: shared errors, or a calibration slope that varies by state. No claim.

## E. C2

**Q19. What does C2 show and why a synthetic probe?**
On a JSON probe with known joint (200 schemas, leader/follower pairs with coupling rho) both errors of Eq. 1 are computed exactly in nats (§5.1). Dependency (A) overtakes estimation (B) only for rho >= 0.5 and several tokens per step: at rho=1, k=8 (confidence) A is 0.338 and B 0.109, 76% of total (§5.2). Confidence ranking raises A, not B (rho=1, k=2: 0.124 vs 0.040 random).
Weak spot: one synthetic distribution and one 110M fine-tune (Limitations); even the sibling fine-tune only partially reads the marker.

**Q20. Why does B change with rho when rho should only move A? And is real text affected by dependency?**
The model reads the marker only in part, so a decoded follower stays broad (error in B), and committed in the same step the missing coupling counts as A; at rho=1, B falls from k=1 to 8 (0.109) while A rises (0 to 0.338) (§5.2). On real text the proxy (parallel-versus-sequential disagreement) is 0.0021 (110M) and 0.0022 (LLaDA) at k=8 (§5.3). Weak spot: the proxy tracks A only weakly (Spearman 0.49 and 0.28) and sees only learned dependence, so it does not show A is small.

## F. C3

**Q21. What is C3a, and what do you have to show for it?**
It commits the n positions with recalibrated confidence >= 1 − alpha (n >= 1) in the model's own order, no extra forward pass, alpha as the speed knob (§6). The calibrated-error guarantee for non-forced commits holds only if the calibrator is monotone in l, which the l x feature terms do not guarantee; in every fit-half state of both tasks it is (share 1.000), and on held-out fit-half documents it cuts commit-confidence ECE from 0.061 to 0.006 (GSM8K) and 0.039 to 0.007 (MBPP), a development check. T12, GSM8K, all 659 report-half problems, at about 4 tokens per step, C3a minus decoder paired over problems: threshold -0.002 [-0.014, 0.012], entropy-bounded -0.005 [-0.035, 0.026], top-k +0.059 [0.026, 0.093], margin +0.053 [0.020, 0.088], PC-Sampler +0.082 [0.050, 0.112]. At about 8 tokens per step the raw threshold is ahead (0.674 against 0.610). KLASS's two faster rows are off its published operating point and carry no comparison (Q22b). An exploratory, teacher-forced check (report-half LLaDA-8B-Instruct decoder states, one commit step replayed) found C3a's unforced commits have per-token error 0.23 to 0.51 x alpha at every alpha, while the raw threshold's is 0.64 to 1.50 x alpha and exceeds alpha at alpha = 0.01 on GSM8K; at matched tokens per state the two differ by at most 0.004 on GSM8K (no speed-accuracy gain).
Weak spot: C3a beats only the fixed-count decoders; against a raw threshold the difference holds 0, so its gain is a calibrated, interpretable knob, not accuracy at speed.

**Q22. Is the C3 comparison fair?**
Knobs are set by speed alone on fit-half problems, never by accuracy; every decoder is compared at its nearest setting within 10%; paired bootstrap on shared problems (§6). But C3a is fitted on teacher-forced states and applied to free generation, one model, greedy, one seed, two tasks, uncorrected multiplicity (Limitations, §6).

**Q22b. Does the harness reproduce published decoders? And what about KLASS?**
Sanity (Appendix C3): 200 fit-half GSM8K problems at Fast-dLLM's settings, 5-shot: top-k 0.835 [0.777, 0.880] against a published 0.793, threshold 0.845 [0.788, 0.889] against 0.792 (Wilson). Both published values lie inside, but our points are 4 to 5 points higher, beyond the plan's 1-2 point bar; 200 problems give intervals about 10 points wide. KLASS: ours keeps the KL history per 32-token block, takes the KL in the reverse direction and uses 32-token blocks; at its published thresholds it runs at about 2.6 tokens per step as published, but to aim at 4 and 8 the pre-set knob path lowers tau to 0.29 and 0.2 (reaching 4.0 and 4.9), far below its published range. Those two rows (dagger) describe that path, not KLASS as intended, and no comparison rests on them. Found after scoring; disclosed in prereg deviation 17.

## G. Limitations and threats to validity

**Q23. Biggest threats to validity?**
Teacher forcing; model and text type confounded; 8B models in fp16 (4-bit checked on LLaDA-Base only, decoder-state interval wide); the field term is only as context-aware as three or four state features (§Limitations); contamination screen catches verbatim recall only; E2 depends on the calibrator's form in 2 of 14 cells; claims are about change with field size (E1, E2), not levels; where levels are read we rely on the 110M arm, and its levels need not transfer to the 8B models.

**Q24. Many analyses are "exploratory". Is the confirmatory part just a handful of contrasts?**
Yes: only E1/E2 contrasts in two families of 14 are claims; decoder k=2, 8, matched uniform states, beta_z, gamma_k, E3, the native gap, controls, robustness runs and the independent estimator carry no claims (§4 "Arms and claims"; Limitations). That is honest. The controls (Table 4, A.2) rule out position, token type and a per-mask-rate temperature as the source of the decoder-state growth, but they are exploratory too.

**Q24b. Was the analysis pre-registered? What changed after the freeze?**
Yes, as an annotated git tag rather than an OSF upload. prereg-v1 (6b4c25e) froze prereg/frozen_config.json and the analysis commands before the report-half runs. prereg-v1.1 (99f8ac3) only adds a warm start to the Eq. 2/3 bootstrap IRLS. On our data it is a speed change only: coefficients agree with the cold start to within 1.3e-12, and --eq-cold-start reproduces v1 exactly (deviation 19). The C1 deviations are listed in prereg/deviations.md:
- 15: Eq. 2/3 intervals (beta_z, gamma) use 1,000 resamples, and the two knot runs 200, so the knot panels are descriptive only. Every E1/E2 interval stays at 10,000.
- 16: an exploratory notebook computed report-half 110M statistics before the tag. Only one person saw them; they were not shared, and the read is disclosed. Four choices were settled afterwards, none depending on data: the two resample counts; the claim rule (with every floor 0 it is the Holm rejection alone, so it cannot change a result); and the family size of 14, where a missing member counts as p = 1, which can only make the test more conservative.
- 18: E1/E2 were run first with --skip-regression, which gives identical E1/E2 numbers.
(The paper says none of this, by choice; answer only if asked. Source: abhiraj's check, 28 Oct.)

## H. Why care

**Q25. Why should a practitioner care, if base-model gaps are about 0.01?**
Because commit-time overconfidence is 0.08 to 0.18 at 32-63 masked positions and 0.15 to 0.27 at 128-255 for instruct models (native gap, teacher-forced k=4 decoder, exploratory, §4.3), and threshold decoders such as Fast-dLLM treat confidence as a probability of being right. It tells decoder designers where a calibrated cutoff has headroom, and C2 tells designers when parallel commits fail from dependency versus marginal error. C3 shows no speed-accuracy gain over a raw threshold, only over fixed-count decoders (GSM8K).

## Top 5 weak spots

1. **C3 is no more accurate than a raw threshold at matched speed (GSM8K: -0.002 [-0.014, 0.012]); it beats top-k, margin and PC-Sampler by 0.05 to 0.08, intervals above 0. The raw threshold is ahead at 8 tokens per step.** Defuse: "C3a is a calibration-preserving rule: unforced commits stay under the target risk, so alpha means something, with no detectable loss in accuracy against the strongest baseline (interval within about 1.4 points); we report the null on speed-accuracy against a threshold. The contribution stands on C1 and C2."
2. **The winner's curse (field term) appears only in the instruct models, in 7 of 8 cells:** all four decoder cells, but not LLaDA-I GSM8K uniform, where E2 is -0.003 [-0.004, -0.003]. In base/110M models |E2| < 0.005 is at the estimator's null level, and the decoder-state growth is mostly own miscalibration. On MBPP and in Dream-I GSM8K uniform states the shared-error term is as large as E2 or larger. Under the additive calibrator the field term is smaller in all eight instruct cells (0 to 67% of its primary size); E2 keeps its sign and declared flag in 12 of 14 cells, the misses being LLaDA-I MBPP uniform (drops to 0) and 110M decoder states (sign flips, at bias level). Defuse: "E2 keeps its sign and declared flag in 12 of 14 cells under a second calibrator form and in 14 of 14 with other knots; all four instruct decoder cells keep theirs. The form changes E2's size, not its sign, except in the two cells we name and do not rely on." 
3. **Teacher forcing, and the instruct versus base confound (text type and model type change together).** Defuse: state that effects are measured in always-right-decoder states and note that only the 110M arm isolates domain.
4. **Statistical design:** the floor is 0 in all three families, so "declared" adds nothing and a claim is the Holm rejection alone (prereg deviation 16c). E2 intervals condition on the fit-half calibrators and exclude the estimator's null-level bias. Claims come only from the two 14-cell families (gamma_k is Holm-corrected but carries no claim). Shared errors (ICC > 0 in every arm) give the split a nonzero third term. Defuse: the floor was measured, not assumed, so there is no run-to-run noise to guard against. Claims rest on the document bootstrap and Holm. Small base E2 values are not read as a curse.
5. **Real-text reading of C2 and the uniform-versus-decoder sign flip: the dependency proxy tracks A only weakly (Spearman 0.49 and 0.28), the probe is one synthetic distribution and one 110M model, and the time-split run does not reproduce the base uniform E1.** Defuse: say that real text supports a direction, not a split in nats, and that the decoder-state positive E1 is the result replicated on unseen text.
